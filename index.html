The Three Flagship Curriculums

CS229 14 Lectures

Machine Learning: Foundations & Modern Paradigms

Instructors: Prof. Christopher Ré & Tengyu Ma • Stanford CS

The canonical foundational machine learning curriculum. Rigorously covers supervised learning, empirical risk minimization, generalized linear models, generative learning algorithms, neural backprop, unsupervised learning (clustering, EM, PCA), and contemporary paradigms including Diffusion Models and LoRA.

Linear & Logistic Regression GLMs GDA & Naive Bayes Backpropagation K-Means & EM PCA Diffusion Models LoRA & RAG

CS336 18 Lectures

Language Modeling from Scratch: The Full LLM Stack

Focus: Systems, Kernels, Scaling & Post-Training • Stanford Spring 2026

A first-principles engineering journey into modern Large Language Models: from BPE tokenization, PyTorch resource accounting, and Transformer variants (MoE, RoPE) through FlashAttention-2, Triton GPU kernels, distributed training (ZeRO/FSDP), scaling laws, data mixtures, and post-training alignment (SFT, DPO, RLVR, GRPO).

Tokenization Transformers & MoE FlashAttention Triton GPU Kernels ZeRO & FSDP Scaling Laws SFT & DPO RLVR & GRPO
Explore CS336 → Lecture 1
CS231N 18 Lectures

Deep Learning for Computer Vision

Focus: Visual Recognition, Generative Vision & 3D • Stanford Spring 2026

Comprehensive study of visual computing from convolutional neural networks and modern residual architectures to Vision Transformers (ViT), object detection, video modeling, distributed visual training, self-supervised representations, generative vision (VAEs, GANs, Diffusion), 3D vision (NeRF), and robot learning.

CNNs & ResNets Vision Transformers (ViT) Detection & Segmentation Video Understanding Self-Supervised Learning Diffusion Models 3D Vision & NeRF Robot Learning
