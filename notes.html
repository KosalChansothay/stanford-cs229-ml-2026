Lecture Notes

All available lectures — CS229 & CS336, Spring 2026

  • Lecture 1: Introduction

    SupervisedUnsupervisedReinforcement

    Course overview, ML paradigms, Mitchell's definition of machine learning, and the three pillars of ML: supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning.

  • Lecture 2: Supervised Learning Setup

    Linear RegressionGradient DescentERM

    Formal setup of supervised learning with linear regression, least squares cost function, empirical risk minimization, and optimization via batch gradient descent and normal equations.

  • Lecture 3: Logistic Regression

    MLEClassificationNewton's Method

    Probabilistic justification of least squares via maximum likelihood estimation, logistic regression with the sigmoid link function, gradient ascent, and the SGD vs. Newton's method trade-off.

  • Lecture 4: Exponential Family, GLMs & Classification

    Exponential FamilyGLMSoftmaxLabel Smoothing

    A unifying framework for distributions (Bernoulli, Gaussian) via the exponential family, the three assumptions of GLMs, softmax regression for multi-class classification, and label smoothing as regularization.

  • Lecture 5: Gaussian Discriminant Analysis

    Generative LearningGDANaive BayesLaplace Smoothing

    The shift from discriminative to generative modeling: Gaussian Discriminant Analysis with closed-form parameter estimation, its connection to logistic regression, Naive Bayes for text classification, and Laplace smoothing.

  • Lecture 6: Dataset Split, ML Advice

    Bias-VarianceRidge RegressionDouble DescentCross-ValidationHyperband

    The bias-variance-noise decomposition, ridge regression and its eigenvalue stabilization, the modern double descent phenomenon, k-fold cross-validation, and compute-efficient hyperparameter tuning with Hyperband.

  • Lecture 7: Neural Networks 1 (Architecture)

    Neural NetworksActivationsMLPResNetLayerNorm

    From linear models to parameterized nonlinear networks: softmax and cross-entropy, why Newton's method fails under non-convexity, activation functions, MLP vectorization, residual connections, LayerNorm/RMSNorm, and convolutions.

  • Lecture 8: Neural Networks 2 (Backpropagation)

    BackpropagationAutodiffJacobianHessianGPU Memory

    Reverse-mode automatic differentiation: the O(N) gradient theorem, the vector chain rule and Jacobians, backward passes for matrix multiplication (Hebbian learning) and activation layers, Hessian-vector products, and activation caching on GPUs.

  • Lecture 9: K-Means and GMM (non-EM)

    UnsupervisedK-MeansGMMK-Means++Jensen's Inequality

    The shift to unlabeled data: K-Means coordinate descent and the distortion function, Gaussian Mixture Models with soft posterior memberships, K-Means++ initialization, and Jensen's inequality as the foundation of the EM lower bound.

  • Lecture 10: GMM (EM) and PCA

    EMELBOPCAEigen DecompositionDimensionality Reduction

    The formal EM algorithm via the ELBO and the Q-distribution trick, monotonic convergence guarantees, and PCA: maximizing projected variance solved by eigenvalue decomposition of the covariance matrix, with mean-centering and rescaling practices.

  • Lecture 11: Diffusion Models

    DiffusionGenerative ModelsELBOScore FunctionsSDEs

    The forward noising process and its closed-form marginals, the learned reverse denoising chain, the ELBO decomposition into per-step Gaussian mean-matching losses, and the continuous-time SDE view where the score function steers generation.

  • Lecture 12: Representation Learning & Foundation Models

    Foundation ModelsLinear ProbingFine-TuningLP-FTLoRA

    The pre-train-then-adapt paradigm: linear probing vs fine-tuning and the destructive gradient problem solved by LP-FT, plus Low-Rank Adaptation — parameter-efficient updates that enable multi-tenant serving of customized models.

  • Lecture 13: Contrastive Learning, Semantic Search & RAG

    EmbeddingsSimCLRContrastive LossVector SearchRAG

    Self-supervised embeddings via SimCLR's NT-Xent loss with a force-balance proof, semantic vector search with approximate nearest neighbors, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation as a secure, modular alternative to fine-tuning.

  • Lecture 14: Transformers & In-Context Learning

    TokenizationBPESelf-AttentionCausal MaskingFlashAttention

    Autoregressive language modeling from BPE tokenization to next-token cross-entropy, single- and multi-head causal self-attention with the -infinity mask trick, and the O(T²) bottleneck that FlashAttention solves with SRAM tiling.

  • CS336 Lecture 1: Overview, Tokenization

    CS336BPETokenizationEfficiencyScaling Laws

    The from-scratch philosophy: Accuracy = Efficiency × Resources, Byte-Pair Encoding from raw bytes up, tokenizer compression trade-offs against quadratic attention cost, the naive O(V·L) encoder bottleneck, and Chinchilla-style scaling heuristics.

  • CS336 Lecture 2: PyTorch & Resource Accounting

    CS336PyTorchFLOPs AccountingMixed PrecisionArithmetic IntensityActivation Checkpointing

    Low-level resource accounting: 6NT training FLOPs derivation (2NT fwd, 4NT bwd), mixed precision (BF16 weights/activations, FP32 optimizer states), arithmetic intensity limits on H100, GEMM vs memory-bound ops, and activation checkpointing.

  • CS336 Lecture 3: Architectures

    CS336TransformersRMSNormSwiGLURoPEGQA

    Modern LLM architectural design: Pre-norm residual highway, RMSNorm memory throughput speedups, SwiGLU gated activations with 2/3 hidden dimension scaling, Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA).

  • CS336 Lecture 4: Attention Alternatives & Mixture of Experts

    CS336Linear AttentionSSMMoEGated Delta NetSparse Attention

    Bypassing the quadratic attention wall: Linear attention duality, State-Space Models (Mamba/S4), Gated Delta Net state tracking, DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), and Mixture of Experts (MoE) routing and load-balancing dynamics.

  • CS336 Lecture 5: GPUs, TPUs, and FlashAttention

    CS336GPU ArchitectureFlashAttentionSRAM TilingQuantizationOnline Softmax

    Hardware accelerator architecture: HBM vs SRAM bandwidth gaps, SIMT execution and warp divergence, FP8/FP4 block-scaled quantization, and the FlashAttention algorithm (SRAM tiling and online softmax).

  • CS336 Lecture 6: Kernels, Triton, XLA

    CS336CUDATritonKernel FusionMemory CoalescingXLA

    Low-level GPU kernel development: CUDA thread block indexing, DRAM memory coalescing, shared memory bank conflicts, writing block-level fused operators in Triton, and XLA/TorchInductor compiler graph optimizations.

  • CS336 Lecture 7: Parallelism (Foundations)

    CS336Distributed TrainingDDPTensor ParallelismAllReduceMegatron-LM

    Foundations of distributed LLM training: Collective communication primitives (All-Reduce ring/tree), Distributed Data Parallel (DDP) gradient synchronization, and Megatron-LM column/row Tensor Parallelism (TP).

  • CS336 Lecture 8: Parallelism (Advanced)

    CS336FSDPZeROPipeline ParallelismSequence Parallelism3D Parallelism

    Scaling beyond single nodes: ZeRO memory sharding stages (ZeRO-1/2/3) and PyTorch FSDP, Pipeline Parallelism (PP) with 1F1B scheduling and bubble minimization, Sequence Parallelism (SP), and 3D parallelism topology mapping.

  • CS336 Lecture 9: Scaling Laws (Foundations)

    CS336Scaling LawsChinchillaKaplanIsoFLOPPower Laws

    Empirical scaling laws: Validation loss power laws (L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β), Kaplan vs Chinchilla correction, the 20:1 token-to-parameter ratio, IsoFLOP profiling, and why inference economics drive overtraining.

  • CS336 Lecture 10: Inference (Execution & Optimization)

    CS336LLM InferenceKV CachePagedAttentionSpeculative DecodingvLLMMLA

    Production inference systems: The memory-bound decoding wall, KV cache sizing and bandwidth consumption, vLLM PagedAttention virtual memory management, Speculative Decoding acceptance math, and DeepSeek MLA latent compression.

  • CS336 Lecture 11: Scaling Laws & Advanced Optimizers

    CS336muPWSD ScheduleMuon OptimizerHyperparameter TransferAdamW

    Hyperparameter scaling and next-gen optimization: Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) for zero-shot LR transfer across model sizes, Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedules, and the Muon optimizer (Newton-Schulz matrix updates).

  • CS336 Lecture 12: Evaluation (Methodology & Contamination)

    CS336EvaluationMMLUElo RatingChatbot ArenaContamination

    Evaluating language models: Construct validity vs operational metrics, log-likelihood vs free-form generation scoring, MMLU/GSM8K/GPQA nuances, Chatbot Arena Elo rating modeling, and n-gram/embedding contamination detection.

  • CS336 Lecture 13: Data (Sources & Datasets)

    CS336Pre-training DataCommon CrawlFineWebC4Synthetic Data

    Pre-training data pipelines: Web scraping realities with Common Crawl, curating datasets from C4 to FineWeb-Edu, domain mixing ratios, legal boundaries of shadow libraries, and the role of synthetic data in pre-training.

  • CS336 Lecture 14: Data (Preprocessing & Mixing)

    CS336Data FilteringMinHashLSHDeduplicationFastTextDomain Mixing

    Data processing algorithms: HTML parsing and noise removal, FastText language identification, MinHash and Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) near-deduplication, classifier-based quality heuristics, and dynamic domain mixing.

  • CS336 Lecture 15: Post-Training (SFT & Preference Optimization)

    CS336Post-TrainingSFTRLHFDPOPreference Optimization

    Post-training alignment: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) mechanics and mode extraction, multi-turn prompt templates, Bradley-Terry reward modeling in classic RLHF (PPO), and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) closed-form loss.

  • CS336 Lecture 16: Post-Training — RLVR (Verifiable Rewards)

    CS336RLVRGRPOReasoning ModelsTest-Time ComputeDeepSeek

    The frontier of post-training: Transitioning from soft preferences to verifiable rewards (RLVR) in math/code, DeepSeek GRPO (group relative baseline without a critic), reward hacking mitigation, and test-time compute scaling.

  • CS336 Lecture 17: Alignment — Multimodality

    CS336MultimodalVLMSigLIPLLaVAVision Transformer

    Extending LLMs to multimodality: Vision tokenization via ViT patches, CLIP vs SigLIP pairwise binary contrastive loss, LLaVA linear projection and connector networks, cross-attention fusion (Flamingo), and visual hallucination alignment.

  • CS336 Guest Lecture: Systems and Alternative Architectures (Dan Fu)

    CS336Guest LectureMega-KernelsThunderKittensDisaggregated ServingLoop Transformers

    Dan Fu's guest lecture on cutting-edge inference systems: Disaggregating prefill and decode clusters, full-layer Mega-Kernels, ThunderKittens hardware-aware CUDA optimization, and Parse stabilized loop transformers.
